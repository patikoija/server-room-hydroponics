# 06 — Nutrients

Masterblend 4-18-38 three-part: **Masterblend + calcium nitrate 15.5-0-0 + Epsom salt**
(magnesium sulfate). Cheap, well understood, and better for leafy greens than the multi-bottle
consumer lines.

If the product on hand is something else, ignore the grams below and **mix to EC by meter**
instead.

## Order of operations: nutrients first, pH last

**The nutrient salts drop the pH on their own**, so pH-adjusting plain water before dosing is
wasted effort and overshoots.

> **Dose → mix → measure → pH.**

## Proportions

Lettuce and leafy greens run at **half** the full "tomato" strength:

| Per | Masterblend 4-18-38 | Calcium nitrate | Epsom salt |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **gallon** | 1 g | 1 g | 0.5 g |
| 20 gal | 20 g | 20 g | 10 g |
| 30 gal | 30 g | 30 g | 15 g |
| 40 gal | 40 g | 40 g | 20 g |

Full strength is 2 : 2 : 1 g/gal at EC ~2.0 — **too hot for lettuce**. Start seedlings lighter
still, around ¾ of the half-strength line.

### No gram scale? Measure by volume

These salts run roughly **5 g per level teaspoon, ±15%** — fine for forgiving, deliberately light
lettuce. Verify with an EC meter if you have one; if not, lean **under**.

| Batch | Calcium nitrate | Masterblend | Epsom |
|---|---|---|---|
| **40 gal** | 8 tsp (2 tbsp + 2 tsp) | 8 tsp | 4 tsp (1 tbsp + 1 tsp) |
| **30 gal** | 6 tsp (2 tbsp) | 6 tsp | 3 tsp (1 tbsp) |

A 0.1 g pocket scale is a few dollars and nutrient dosing recurs on every reservoir change, so
volume is a stopgap rather than a plan.

## Mixing

1. **Fill the reservoir to a marked line and record the gallons.** Dose to *that* volume and
   reuse the same line every batch for reproducibility. Fill high enough that a full flood of the
   plumbed buckets never drops the level below the pump intake — ~30 gal is comfortable
   easy-maths for the 6-bucket configuration.
2. **Dissolve the three parts separately, in this order, each fully dissolved before the next:**
   **calcium nitrate → Masterblend → Epsom.**
   **Never combine the concentrated powders directly** — calcium plus the sulfate/phosphate
   precipitates out, which is nutrient lockout. Diluted into the full reservoir with mixing
   between additions they are perfectly fine.
3. **Circulate a few minutes** — run the fill pump to stir, or a circulation pump — so it is
   uniform and the pH settles.
4. **Measure strength in EC: target ~1.2 mS/cm (1200 µS/cm)** for lettuce, ~1.6–1.8 for basil.
   Adjust with more solution (up) or water (down). Measure after mixing, at solution temperature.
5. **Now pH to 5.8–6.0** with pH Down (phosphoric acid), a little at a time, stir, re-measure.
   Overshooting is easy.
6. **Re-check pH after ~30 minutes and again the next day.** It drifts as it equilibrates, and
   tap water's alkalinity pushes it back up, so expect to re-nudge over the first couple of days.

## Use EC, not ppm

The meter on hand reads **EC directly**, which removes the whole ppm-scale ambiguity. If you ever
use a ppm-only pen, the number depends on which scale it uses:

- EC 1.2 ≈ **600 ppm** on a 500 / NaCl meter
- EC 1.2 ≈ **840 ppm** on a 700 / KCl meter

So "target 800 ppm" on a 500-scale pen is actually EC 1.6 — too hot for lettuce. This is a
genuinely common way to cook a crop while believing you followed the recipe.

## Targets at a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| EC, lettuce and leafy | **1.0–1.2 mS/cm** |
| EC, basil | 1.6–1.8 mS/cm |
| EC, seedlings | ~¾ of the leafy figure |
| pH | **5.8–6.0** (acceptable 5.8–6.2) |
| Reservoir working level, 6 buckets | ~30 gal |

## Beneficials

Botanicare Hydroguard (a *Bacillus* inoculant) is cheap insurance against pythium at warm root
temperatures, which a server room guarantees. First dose here was **40 ml into a ~20 gallon
working level**.

**Start it during the dry stage rather than at first mix** — the inoculant wants to colonise the
root zone before the pathogen does, and the dry stage is when the roots are most exposed.

## Topping up versus changing

Reservoir pH drifts up ~0.5 over three days with a crop drawing on it, and EC falls as the plants
take nutrient out faster than they take water. Top up with plain water to the marked line, then
re-measure EC and pH rather than re-dosing blind — a reservoir that gets nutrient added on every
top-up climbs in salinity until something tipburns.
